Privacy Policy — Vazhe (واژه)

Last updated: 26 October 2026 · فارسی

This Privacy Policy applies to Vazhe (واژه), an English⇄Persian dictionary and learning app for Android (package app.vazhe.dictionary) and its web version, published by GiTi Tech ("we", "us", "our"). You can reach us at vazhe.app@gmail.com.

1. Summary

  • We, the developer, do not receive your personal data. The app has no accounts, no sign-in, no servers that store user data, no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking of our own. We never receive your searches, progress, photos or voice.
  • Your history, favorites, wordbook, lessons, quiz and practice results and settings stay on your device only.
  • The Android app shows ads provided by Google AdMob. Google's advertising software may collect certain device information, such as your advertising ID and IP address (section 5). Where the law requires it, the app asks for your choice first.
  • The app uses the internet for ads and to download translation language models once (about 60 MB). Everything else works offline.
  • Camera, microphone and notifications are optional. The app asks for each permission only when you use that feature.
  • You can delete your local data yourself at any time (section 9).

2. Information we do not collect

We, the developer, do not collect or receive: your name, email address, phone number, address, account or login details, payment information, location, contacts, calendar, photos, files, camera images, voice recordings, search queries, learning results, usage analytics, or any other personal information from the app. The exceptions are messages and certificate requests that you choose to email to us (see sections 4 and 10). Third-party software inside the app may handle some data itself, as described in sections 4 and 5.

3. Information stored on your device

To work, the app keeps the following only inside the app's private storage on your device:

  • your settings (interface language, theme, text size, speech rate, reminder choices),
  • your history of viewed words, your favorite words, your personal wordbook, your category names and your flashcard progress,
  • your lesson progress, quiz and practice results, pronunciation scores, reading and listening results, study sessions and streaks,
  • a record of whether you requested a certificate, and a certificate ID derived from your device (section 4).

This data is created and used locally at your request. It is never uploaded, synced or sent to us, and we cannot access, recover or restore it. The app opts out of Android's automatic cloud backup, so the app does not copy this data to cloud backups.

4. Features that involve other software or your own email

  • Pronunciation (text-to-speech). When you tap a speaker button, the app hands the English word to the text-to-speech engine you selected in your Android settings (for example Google or Samsung). That engine is a separate system component governed by its vendor's privacy policy.
  • Speech recognition (microphone). Pronunciation practice and the voice translator use the speech recognition service selected on your device, which is commonly Google's. The app asks for microphone permission only when you first tap the microphone. Depending on your device and its settings, the service may process the audio on your device or send it to its provider under that provider's privacy policy. Vazhe does not record, save or upload your audio.
  • Camera and text recognition. Camera translation uses Google ML Kit text recognition, which runs on your device. The app asks for camera permission only when you first use it. Vazhe does not save or upload your photos.
  • Translation models. Translation uses Google ML Kit on-device translation. The language models (about 60 MB) are downloaded from Google through Google Play services over the internet and then work offline. Google ML Kit may collect basic usage and performance information, as described at developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure.
  • Reminders (notifications). Reminders are optional. They are scheduled locally on your device, use no push server, and the app asks for notification permission only when you turn them on.
  • Certificate of completion. When you request a certificate, the app prepares an email in your own email app. It contains the name you typed, the level, your learning statistics (for example lessons completed, quiz average, number of practice answers and pronunciation average), a certificate ID and a verification code. The certificate ID is a one-way hash derived from a device identifier, so that clearing the app's data does not create a new ID; the raw device identifier is never shown, stored in the email or sent. We receive this information only if you press send in your email app. We use it only to check eligibility and to issue and verify the certificate, keep it only as long as needed for that, and delete it when you ask.
  • Backup export and import. The wordbook export creates a JSON file only when you tap the button. It contains the words, categories, favorites and flashcard progress you saved — not your identity. It is passed to the Android share sheet, where you choose where it goes; after that, the receiving app's privacy policy applies. The file is not encrypted. Import only reads a file you choose and uploads nothing.
  • Links that open outside the app. Some screens contain links (for example to this policy, license texts, Wiktionary, Tatoeba). Tapping one opens your browser, and that website may receive technical information such as your IP address under its own privacy policy.
  • Android System WebView. The app draws its screens with the Android System WebView component supplied with your device. It is part of the operating system.
  • Google Play. If you install the app from Google Play, Google processes information about your download and device under Google's own privacy policy. Google may give us aggregated, non-identifying statistics (for example installs by country, or crash and performance reports from users who chose to share diagnostics with Google).
  • Web version. The web version shows no ads and does not load Google AdMob. It stores the same local data in your browser's storage and caches the dictionary files in your browser. If you use camera or voice features in a browser, they rely on your browser's permission prompts, and some browsers send speech to their vendor's servers. The server that hosts the page may keep standard technical logs under its own policy; we do not receive or analyze them.
  • This policy page. This page is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may process technical data about visitors under GitHub's privacy statement; we do not receive that data.

5. Advertising (Android app)

  • Provider. The Android app shows ads through Google AdMob: banner ads on browsing screens, occasional full-screen ads, and an optional rewarded video that you choose to watch to get an ad-free day. The app does not show ads while you answer questions, and shows fewer ads when you are new.
  • What Google may collect. To serve, measure and secure ads, Google's advertising software may collect and use your advertising ID, IP address (and an approximate location derived from it), device and app information (such as model, operating system version, app version and language), and ad interaction and diagnostic data. It may use cookies or similar technologies. We do not receive this data. We receive only aggregated reports (such as impressions and estimated earnings).
  • Your choice. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and other places where the law requires it, the app shows Google's consent message before requesting ads, and you can change your choice later in Settings → Ad privacy choices, when that option is shown. You can also reset your advertising ID or turn off ad personalization in your Android settings (Settings → Privacy or Google → Ads; the exact path depends on your device).
  • Learn more. How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services · Google Privacy Policy · Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure.

6. Sharing and selling

We do not sell, rent or trade personal information, and we hold no profile of you. We do not use your information for profiling, automated decision-making or training AI models, and we collect no sensitive personal information. Advertising through Google may count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioral advertising or as "targeted advertising" under some US state laws, even though we do not receive the data; where required, you can opt out through the consent message and your Android settings (section 5).

7. Legal basis and our role

For data that stays on your device, you create and control it. For advertising, Google processes data under its own privacy policy and, in the EEA and UK, acts as an independent controller; where consent is required, it is asked through Google's consent message. For emails and certificate requests that you send us, we act as the controller, and our basis is your request and our legitimate interest in replying and preventing duplicate certificates.

8. Children

Vazhe is a general-audience learning app and is not directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where local law sets a higher age). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The app is not part of Google Play's "Designed for Families" program. A dictionary can contain words that some people find offensive. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

9. Your choices, access and deletion

  • Delete in the app: Settings → Delete all my data removes history, favorites, wordbook, categories, progress, results and settings. You can also clear only some items inside the app.
  • Delete from your device: Android Settings → Apps → Vazhe → Storage → Clear data, or uninstall the app.
  • Permissions and reminders: change them in Android Settings → Apps → Vazhe → Permissions, or turn reminders off in the app's Settings.
  • Ads: see section 5.
  • Web version: clear the site's data in your browser settings.
  • Files and emails you sent: exported backup files are ordinary files you control. To delete a certificate request or any message you emailed to us, write to us.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, California and other US states, Canada, Brazil, Australia and other countries), you may have rights over your personal data, such as to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to processing, move your data, withdraw consent, opt out of sale, sharing or targeted advertising, and to complain to your data-protection authority. We hold only what you email us, so write to us to use these rights for that data. For data processed by Google for ads, use Google's tools and the controls in section 5. Data on your device is under your direct control (section 9). We never discriminate against anyone for exercising privacy rights.

If you email us, we receive your email address and message. We use them only to answer you, keep them no longer than needed, and do not share them.

11. Security

We reduce risk by design: no accounts and no servers that store user data mean there is no database of user data to breach. Local data is protected by the Android app sandbox and your device's own security (screen lock and storage encryption); we recommend keeping them enabled. No method is perfectly secure, and exported backup files and emails you send are your responsibility.

12. International users

We do not transfer personal data across borders. Google may process advertising data in the United States and other countries where it operates, under its own policies.

13. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

We do not track you ourselves, so we do not need to react to Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signals. To control advertising, use the choices in section 5.

The app code is MIT-licensed. The dictionary data comes from open sources (Wiktionary via kaikki.org, Tatoeba, Princeton WordNet) and the Vazirmatn font; these are bundled inside the app and never fetched at run time. Attributions and licenses are in the app (Settings → About & licenses) and in NOTICE.md. The other bundled libraries (Capacitor and its plugins) run on your device and do not collect data themselves.

15. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy, for example if the app changes. The "last updated" date at the top shows the current version, and earlier versions remain available in the project's public history. If a change affects how data is collected or used, we will update the policy, the Google Play Data safety form and the in-app text before shipping it.
